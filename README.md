## The rules

### Swing for the fence

In my little corner of the garden, I learned that what I could accomplish often exceeded what people expected.

That taught me to try.

There will always be someone saying we can’t do something. Sometimes they have evidence worth hearing. Sometimes they are describing the boundaries of their own imagination.

Listen carefully. Question your assumptions. Keep your ambition.

We want to eliminate violence, poverty and injustice—and give people back about twenty hours of their working week.

Here, **justice means prevention**. The measure is whether fewer people are harmed.

These are enormous goals. We can still begin now. With the next decision, the next useful tool, the next person we help.

### Understand the system

We are born into groups, raised in groups, and live within systems.

I’m less interested in asking **“Who is the problem?”** than **“What is producing this outcome?”**

Look underneath: incentives, feedback loops, power, information flows, unintended consequences, and assumptions everyone has stopped questioning.

When good people keep producing bad outcomes, investigate what makes those outcomes likely.

If humans built the systems we live inside, humans can redesign them.

### Automate what costs us life

Use automation, machine learning and whatever else earns its place.

Make it easier to build, run, improve, connect and share. Pay attention to whose time is saved and whose work becomes harder.

Efficiency should leave people with more room to live.

### Learn without humiliation

When something fails, make it possible to tell the truth.

What happened? What conditions allowed it? What would prevent it happening again?

Responsibility includes changing those conditions. A useful post-mortem leaves the system better and the people more able to speak.

### Build like a sailor

Observe carefully. Have a direction. Reconsider the route when the wind changes.

Let go of self-doubt without losing the ability to question your decisions.

Choose your crew with care. Make things people can adopt without having to turn their whole world upside down.

### Remember the person

Before reducing someone to the worst thing they have done, try to picture the life that brought them here.

The child they were. What they learned. What they feared. What the people before them handed down.

Understanding does not erase harm. It can help us find a way to prevent more of it.

## I am into systems

My background crosses anthropology, psychology, technology, governance and social systems.

I like first principles, uncomfortable questions, open systems, and solutions that reduce the need for control.

I don’t have all the answers. I’m interested in what becomes possible when we ask better questions—and build something with the answers.
